# How Brighton Homeowners Can Keep Pipes From Freezing This Winter

Brighton sits at about 5,000 feet on the Front Range, and January overnight lows average around 23°F. Pipe-freeze research behind American Red Cross guidance puts the danger point at about 20°F outdoors or below, especially for exposed and uninsulated pipes in unheated spaces — a mark Brighton passes regularly.

The prevention list is short and cheap. Insulate every exposed run you can reach — basement, crawlspace, garage, attic — and start with hose bibs and pipes in exterior walls. Disconnect hoses and drain hose bibs before the first hard freeze, which around Brighton typically arrives in early October. Seal gaps where pipes pass through exterior walls, and keep the thermostat at 55°F or higher when you are away. On the coldest nights, let vulnerable faucets drip and open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.
