# After the Plumber Leaves: Drying Out and Checking for Hidden Damp

The pipe is fixed, the water is off the floor - and the job is only half done. The damage that costs money later is the water you cannot see: soaked into the subfloor, wicked up inside the wall cavity, sitting in the insulation. Drying has to be deliberate. Open the room up, run fans across wet surfaces rather than at them, and use a dehumidifier with the doors closed so it pulls moisture out of materials, not just the air. Check the hidden places over the next week: skirting boards swelling, a musty smell that was not there before, paint bubbling low on the wall, floorboards starting to lift. Caught in the first days, hidden damp is a drying job. Found in three months, it is a mould and repair job. The leak takes an hour to fix; the drying takes days - and it is the days that decide the final bill.
