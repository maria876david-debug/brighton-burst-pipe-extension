# Going Away This Winter? How to Stop a Burst Pipe Ruining an Empty Brighton House

The worst burst pipes in Brighton do not happen when you are home. They happen in an empty house, in the middle of a cold snap, where the water runs for days before anyone opens the front door. A burst pipe repair in Brighton typically runs $200–$3,000 with an average around $500 — but that average assumes someone caught it quickly. In an empty house, the water damage bill, not the pipe bill, is the disaster.

## Before you lock the door

**Turn the water off at the stopcock if you can.** For a trip of more than a few days in freezing weather, this is the single most protective step: no water pressure means no flood, whatever the temperature does. Know where your stopcock is and check it turns before the day you travel.

**If the water stays on, keep the heating alive.** Set the heating to come on low — enough to keep the house above freezing, especially overnight. A timer burst morning and evening is far cheaper than a flooded kitchen.

**Lag the vulnerable pipes.** Pipes in the loft, the garage and against outside walls freeze first. Foam lagging costs very little and takes an afternoon; do it before the trip, not after the warning.

**Open the loft hatch a crack.** Warm air from the house drifting into the loft keeps the coldest pipes a few degrees safer.

**Consider a leak alarm.** A battery water alarm costs very little, sits on the floor near the water heater, washing machine or under-sink pipes, and shrieks the moment it gets wet. In an occupied house it saves hours; paired with a neighbour who has a key, it turns a silent flood into a phone call. Some smart versions send an alert to your phone wherever you are — worth it for a house that will sit empty through the coldest weeks.

## Ask a human to be your alarm

Technology helps, but a neighbour or family member with a key, popping in twice a week, beats every gadget. A leak caught on day one is a mop and a plumber visit. The same leak caught on day ten is new floors, new plaster and weeks of drying. Leave your plumber's number with whoever checks the house — and on the fridge.
