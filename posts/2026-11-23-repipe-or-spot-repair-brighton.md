# Repipe or Spot Repair? How to Tell When a Brighton Home Needs More Than a Patch

Every homeowner who's fixed the same pipe twice asks the same question on the third leak: are we repairing a pipe, or feeding a system that's finished? The two jobs live in different price worlds, so the answer matters. Here's how plumbers actually draw the line.

## What each option costs

A spot repair in the Brighton area typically runs $200–$3,000, with most jobs landing around $500 — open the wall or ceiling, cut out the failed section, fit new pipe, close up. Repiping is priced by length instead: roughly $150–$250 per linear foot, because you're replacing runs of pipe through the house rather than one failed joint. A call-out to assess the situation usually runs $100–$300, credited against the picture it gives you.

## Signs a patch is still the right call

- It's the first leak in that line, and the pipe around it looks sound.
- The cause was a one-off event — a freeze burst in an uninsulated run you can now protect.
- The rest of the house holds pressure and the water runs clear.

A single failure in an otherwise healthy system is bad luck, not a pattern. Fix it, insulate it, move on.

## Signs you're looking at a repipe

- **Leaks in different places.** The second and third leaks aren't new problems; they're the same pipe ageing at the same rate everywhere.
- **Discoloured water.** Rusty or yellow water from several taps points to corrosion along the whole run, not one bad joint.
- **Falling pressure year on year.** Scale and corrosion narrow old pipe — galvanised lines especially — until showers fade when a tap opens elsewhere.
- **The pipe itself.** Galvanised steel at 40-plus years and old polybutylene are materials plumbers flag on sight. The question stops being "if" and becomes "before or after the ceiling comes down?"

## The middle step most people skip

Before committing either way, get the system assessed: pressure test, a look at the pipe material in the basement or crawlspace, and an honest map of what's been repaired already. That visit costs a call-out fee and replaces a guess with a plan — including phased repiping, where the worst runs go first and the rest follow when budget allows.
