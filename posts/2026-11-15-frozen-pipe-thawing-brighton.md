# Frozen Pipe in Brighton? How to Thaw It Safely — and What Never to Do

You turn on the faucet on a bitter morning and nothing comes out — or a thin trickle that fades to nothing. In Brighton, where January overnight lows average around 23°F, that is a frozen pipe until proven otherwise. The good news: a frozen pipe is not yet a burst pipe. Thawed carefully, many come through fine.

Start by opening the affected faucet just a crack, so melting water has somewhere to go. Then apply gentle, patient heat: a hair dryer moved slowly along the pipe, warm towels refreshed as they cool, or a space heater warming the area — kept well clear of anything flammable, and never left unattended. Work from the faucet end back toward the blockage. Thawing usually takes 30 minutes to a few hours.

Never use an open flame — no propane torch, no blowtorch — and never pour boiling water over a frozen pipe. Both can crack the pipe you are trying to save. And keep your main shutoff valve in mind the whole time: the moment flow returns is the moment you find out whether the pipe held. Drips, hissing inside a wall, or a new stain mean it did not — shut the main valve off and call (720) 918-0220.
